# Brand note — Redline

Direction: **Clinical trust**. The product's entire value proposition is that it never says more than the document supports (ADR-0001, ADR-0007) — the visual identity should read the same way: precise and restrained, not loud.

## Palette
- **Ink** `#1C2733` — primary text, primary background in dark contexts
- **Paper** `#F7F4EE` — primary background, warm off-white rather than clinical white
- **Slate** `#5B6572` — secondary text, borders, structure
- **Flag** `#B3452E` — muted rust-red, reserved *only* for an actual severity flag. Never decorative, never used for branding chrome. If it shows up, it means something was found.

## Tone
Confident about what the document says, hedged about what it means — same rule as the product's own copy (ADR-0007). No exclamation points, no alarm language, no red anywhere except an actual flag.

## Tagline
"Know what you're signing."

## Why this direction, not the others considered
Two other directions were considered: an "editorial red-pen" look leaning fully into the name's literal metaphor, and a "calm advocate" look using warm teal/green to soften the tone. Both were passed over — the red-pen direction risks reading as alarmist for a product whose whole premise is restraint, and the advocate direction drifts too far from what the product actually does (cite and rank, not reassure). Clinical trust was chosen because it's the direction that most directly reflects the product's actual behavior rather than what its name suggests it should look like.
