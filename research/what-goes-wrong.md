# What Goes Wrong: Recurring Clause Types That Burn People in Contracts

## What was searched

This is pre-PRD research for Redline (a contract-review tool). Goal: find evidence-backed
recurring clause patterns that cause real financial or legal harm — not a generic legal
checklist. Sources pulled from FTC rulemakings/press releases, state AG litigation, legal-blog
analyses of complaint patterns, Amazon/PayPal seller forums, and freelancer-industry writing.

10 web searches were run (of a 12 cap) covering: auto-renewal/negative-option billing,
arbitration/class-action waivers, freelance IP assignment, non-compete clauses, personal
guarantees, indemnification, limitation-of-liability caps, junk fees/price escalators,
unilateral termination/account suspension, and freelance scope creep/unlimited revisions.
2 of a 15-page fetch budget were used to verify claims in depth (a freelance contract
red-flags cheat sheet, and the NY AG's Northern Leasing press release). Research stopped
once 10 distinct sourced findings were reached, past the 8-finding floor set for this task.

No ranking beyond "confidence in evidence volume" should be read as a severity ranking of
dollar harm — the research did not attempt to quantify harm in comparable units across
categories, and says so explicitly per item below.

## Ranked findings

**Ranking basis and confidence:** Ranked roughly by (a) how much regulatory/legal-action
evidence exists (a proxy for "this is common enough that agencies and courts have acted") and
(b) whether the evidence includes concrete harm patterns vs. just structural description.
Confidence in this ordering is **low-to-moderate** — the underlying searches surface what's
been written about, not a representative sample of all contract disputes, and freelance-specific
items in particular rely on advocacy/practitioner blogs rather than agency data. Treat the top
6 as "clearly evidenced as widespread," and items 7-10 as "real and recurring, but with thinner
evidence on true frequency."

### 1. Auto-renewal / negative-option billing
**Harm pattern:** Consumers are enrolled (often via a "free trial") and then billed on a
recurring basis without a clear, easy way to cancel; cancellation flows are frequently designed
to be harder than sign-up (multi-step, phone-only, retention gauntlets).
**Frequency signal:** Very common — described by the FTC as generating "more than 100,000
complaints over the past five years," with the agency issuing a Negative Option Rule and a 2026
Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, plus enforcement actions against Match.com, Chegg, and
Amazon specifically over deceptive billing/cancellation practices.
**Sources:**
- https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/p064202_negative_option_rule.pdf
- https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/getting-and-out-free-trials-auto-renewals-and-negative-option-subscriptions
- https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/alerts-practices-ba-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-gets-new-life
- https://www.powellmajestro.com/negative-option-billing-the-auto-renewal-trap-costing-billions/

### 2. Arbitration clauses / class-action waivers
**Harm pattern:** Buried in boilerplate (often mid-document or on the back of a page), these
clauses force disputes into private, often confidential, individual arbitration and strip the
ability to join a class action — which in practice means most consumers with small-dollar
claims never pursue them at all, effectively immunizing the company for widespread but low-value
harm.
**Frequency signal:** Very common — appears across credit cards, cell phone contracts, online
retail, social platforms, rideshare apps, and product warranties; described by legal
commentators as standard "take it or leave it" boilerplate in consumer contracts.
**Sources:**
- https://www.thelyonfirm.com/blog/arbitration-clauses-user-agreements-class-actions
- https://dicellolevitt.com/arguing-class-actions-arbitration-and-class-action-waivers-the-hypocrisy-of-the-textualists/
- https://www.superlawyers.com/resources/consumer-law/how-mandatory-arbitration-clauses-impact-consumer-rights/

### 3. Non-compete / TRAP (Training Repayment Agreement Provision) clauses
**Harm pattern:** Restricts workers — including low-wage workers, not just executives — from
taking new jobs in their field; FTC findings describe non-competes as derailing careers,
destroying finances, and suppressing wages even for workers not directly bound by one. TRAPs
(a "de facto non-compete") force workers to pay their employer just to quit.
**Frequency signal:** Very common — the FTC estimated roughly 30 million U.S. workers were bound
by a non-compete, prompting a 2024 nationwide ban rule (with ongoing litigation/rulemaking
activity into 2026).
**Sources:**
- https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/01/ftc-proposes-rule-ban-noncompete-clauses-which-hurt-workers-harm-competition
- https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/04/ftc-takes-action-against-noncompete-agreements-securing-protections-workers
- https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/05/07/2024-09171/non-compete-clause-rule

### 4. Personal guarantees (commercial leases, small-business financing/equipment leases)
**Harm pattern:** A business owner signs personally as guarantor; when the business fails, the
landlord/lessor can pursue the owner's personal assets — bank accounts, home equity — not just
the business's. Documented real cases include a couple sued jointly for $90,000 after their
business failed, and a business owner sued personally for over $100,000 in unpaid rent and CAM
fees after a lease default. Separately, NY's AG won a suit against Northern Leasing Systems,
which used deceptive personal-guarantee-backed equipment leases against small businesses
(flower shops, restaurants, salons): **29,000+ default judgments and 5,600+ complaints filed
since 2010**, over 95% against out-of-state owners unable to defend themselves; the court
vacated the judgments, banned the company from leasing, and held its CEO/VP personally liable.
**Frequency signal:** Common in commercial leasing/equipment financing; the Northern Leasing case
shows this can scale into a mass-fraud pattern, not just isolated bad luck.
**Sources:**
- https://harrisonlawaz.com/business/personal-guarantees-in-commercial-leases-what-you-should-know/
- https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2020/attorney-general-james-wins-lawsuit-against-northern-leasing-systems-delivering

### 5. Indemnification clauses (esp. "defend and indemnify," uncapped/broad scope)
**Harm pattern:** Larger counterparties push liability onto smaller ones with less leverage. A
"defend" obligation requires paying the other side's legal fees from day one of any claim,
before fault is determined — so a smaller party can be on the hook for a large company's
negligence. Commercial general liability insurance often excludes contractual indemnification,
leaving the indemnifying party uninsured for exactly this risk. A concrete illustration from a
freelance-contract analysis: on a $12,000 project, a single meritless third-party claim can
exceed the entire project fee before the merits are even reached.
**Frequency signal:** Frequently mentioned in small-business and contractor legal guidance as a
recurring source of unexpected liability, especially in subcontracting/construction and
freelance/agency work.
**Sources:**
- https://mhslaw.com/the-hidden-danger-in-your-contracts-indemnification-clauses-that-can-sink-a-small-business/
- https://blog.ecbm.com/how-an-indemnification-clause-in-a-contract-can-causes-problems-for-your-business
- https://cheatsheets.davidveksler.com/contract-red-flags.html

### 6. Limitation-of-liability caps (esp. asymmetric caps and low fixed-dollar caps)
**Harm pattern:** A vendor's liability is capped (commonly at 12 months of fees paid), while the
customer's own liability may be uncapped — or a cap is set as a token fixed dollar amount (e.g.,
$100 on a $500,000 deal) that courts may find unconscionable. Legal analysis notes that a broad
indemnity promise is "effectively worthless" if the liability cap sitting next to it is very low.
Limitation-of-liability and termination-rights clauses are cited as the two most frequently
disputed provisions when a SaaS relationship breaks down.
**Frequency signal:** Common in SaaS/vendor contracts; freelance-contract guidance separately
flags asymmetric caps (client's liability capped, contractor's is not) as a recurring red flag.
**Sources:**
- https://www.hyperstart.com/blog/limitation-of-liability/
- https://www.cloudnuro.ai/blog/saas-liability-cap
- https://cheatsheets.davidveksler.com/contract-red-flags.html

### 7. Junk fees / hidden fees / price-increase clauses
**Harm pattern:** Mandatory fees disclosed only at the end of a purchase flow (or buried in
contract terms allowing later price increases), inflating the real price well beyond what was
advertised. FTC's rulemaking record cites a specific complaint example: a $299.11 "processing
fee" on contact lenses that raised the total price from $271.92 to $579.98 — more than doubling
the advertised price.
**Frequency signal:** Very common in ticketing, hotels, utilities, and rental housing per the
FTC; the FTC's junk-fee rulemaking drew over 60,000 public comments, with roughly 48,000 of
those explicitly supporting a crackdown.
**Sources:**
- https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/r207011unfairjunkfeesnprmfinal.pdf
- https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/10/2024-30293/trade-regulation-rule-on-unfair-or-deceptive-fees

### 8. Unilateral termination / account suspension clauses ("terminate for any reason, without notice")
**Harm pattern:** Platforms reserve sole-discretion rights to suspend or terminate access "for
any reason" and "without prior notice," sometimes freezing funds along with the account.
Real-world complaint evidence from Amazon seller forums describes accounts terminated with only
automated responses, ignored appeals despite submitted evidence, and funds held for extended
periods after termination (one case cited: UK/EU account terminated in Dec. 2022, still unpaid
10 months after approval to release the hold). PayPal freezes/rolling reserves are described as
a common way a sudden account action can shut down a small business's cash flow entirely.
**Frequency signal:** Common as a contract structure (appears in Firstrade, 500px, and gaming
platform terms per the search); real complaint volume is harder to size precisely, but Amazon
itself is reported to have terminated over 3 million seller accounts in 2023 for policy
violations, which gives a rough scale of how often the clause is actually invoked.
**Sources:**
- https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/d748febd-ad64-40d5-9d0c-1201b87c84c1
- https://www.traverselegal.com/blog/your-amazon-or-paypal-account-was-frozen-by-a-trademark-lawsuit-a-sellers-guide-to-schedule-a-cases/
- https://www.amazonsellers.attorney/paypal-appeals.html

### 9. IP assignment in freelance / work-for-hire contracts
**Harm pattern:** Client contracts often use "work made for hire" language even when the work
doesn't legally qualify as such under the Copyright Act's nine-category test — functioning as a
broad, possibly overreaching assignment. Common problem patterns flagged by practitioner
analysis: ownership transferring to the client *before* full payment is received, and assignment
language sweeping in a freelancer's pre-existing tools/reusable code libraries, not just the
deliverable — both of which restrict a freelancer's ability to reuse their own work or build a
portfolio.
**Frequency signal:** Described as one of the most common freelance-contract red flags in
practitioner guidance; no aggregate complaint-count data was found (see Could Not Find, below).
**Sources:**
- https://cheatsheets.davidveksler.com/contract-red-flags.html
- https://www.workwiseforscreen.org.uk/en/topics/contracts/modules/module/resources/ip-clauses-in-work-for-hire-with-freelancers-what-you-need-to-know
- https://clauseshield.app/blog/ip-ownership-clauses-freelancers

### 10. Scope creep / unlimited-revisions clauses (freelance-specific)
**Harm pattern:** "Unlimited revisions" language, or vaguely defined project scope, lets a
client keep requesting changes indefinitely without additional pay — an anecdote cited in
freelance-industry writing describes a homepage project reaching "revision 47" six months after
signing under an unlimited-revisions term. Practitioner guidance uniformly recommends capping
revisions (commonly 2-3 rounds) and defining an explicit scope/finish line in the contract.
**Frequency signal:** Frequently mentioned across freelance-industry blogs and guides as a
recurring complaint pattern. Note: one source surfaced statistics attributed to a "2026
Freelancers Union study" (67% doing unpaid work due to scope creep) and a "2024 project
management survey" (52% of projects affected); these could not be independently verified against
a primary source within this research's budget, so they are flagged here as **unverified** and
excluded from the evidence claim above — treat them as unconfirmed until traced to source.
**Sources:**
- https://neshawoolery.com/blog/control-scope-creep
- https://www.plutio.com/freelancer-magazine/scope-creep
- https://cheatsheets.davidveksler.com/contract-red-flags.html

## Could not find (gaps)

- **Aggregate complaint-volume data for freelance-specific clauses** (IP assignment, scope
  creep, net-60/90 payment terms, asymmetric indemnification/liability caps). Unlike the
  consumer-side categories (which have FTC complaint counts), freelance-contract harm evidence
  in this research came from practitioner/advocacy blogs and anecdotes, not a regulator or
  survey body with a transparent methodology. The "67% of freelancers" and "52% of projects"
  scope-creep statistics found in search results could not be traced to a verifiable primary
  source and are explicitly flagged as unverified above.
- **Direct Reddit/forum thread evidence for freelance IP assignment** — search results surfaced
  legal-practitioner analysis instead of an accessible complaint community would allow.
- **Tenant/lease-specific complaint volume with numbers** — found general guidance on early
  termination fees and rent-increase disputes (e.g., Philadelphia Fair Housing Commission on
  unfair rental practices), but did not find an FTC/HUD-scale aggregate complaint count
  comparable to the auto-renewal or non-compete figures, so no frequency claim is made for
  general lease terms beyond personal-guarantee-backed commercial leases.
- **Fee-escalator clauses specifically embedded in long-term service/subscription contracts**
  (as opposed to point-of-sale junk fees) — the junk-fees finding above documents point-of-sale
  hidden fees well, but did not surface strong evidence specifically about mid-contract price-
  escalation clauses (e.g., "we may raise your rate at renewal") as a distinct complaint pattern.
- No additional clause categories beyond the seed list and the two added above (junk
  fees/price-at-checkout, and unilateral termination/account suspension) turned up strong enough
  evidence within the search budget to include as a distinct finding.
